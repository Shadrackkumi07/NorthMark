Northmark Facility Services writes a separate cleaning scope for each type of building we serve, because a dental practice, a bank branch, a distribution center and a church do not share the same risks, schedules or standards. The pages below explain how we approach each industry in Fargo, West Fargo and Moorhead: what we clean, what we leave to specialists, how we handle access and what a typical schedule looks like.

## Why industry matters in commercial cleaning

A generic cleaning checklist treats every building as an office. That works poorly in practice:

- **Healthcare spaces** need clear separation between what a janitorial team handles and what clinical staff handle, plus respect for patient privacy.
- **Financial institutions** need cleaning that fits around vaults, cash handling areas and strict access rules.
- **Multi-tenant buildings** need consistent common-area standards and coordination with many tenants.
- **Warehouses and plants** need janitorial work that fits around production schedules, forklifts and safety rules.
- **Schools, childcare centers and churches** need schedules that respect irregular use and the people who use the building.

Our [how we work](/how-we-work) model stays the same in every building: walkthrough, written scope, secure onboarding, checklist visits, supervisor QA and one contact. What changes is what goes into the scope.

## How we approach specialized environments honestly

Industry pages are easy to write and hard to live up to. We hold ourselves to a simple rule: **we describe what our janitorial and maintenance teams actually do in each building type, and we say clearly what belongs to someone else.**

For example, in [surgical centers](/industries/surgical-centers) our scope covers waiting rooms, offices, restrooms, staff areas and corridors. Terminal cleaning of operating rooms and sterile processing remains with the center's trained environmental services staff unless the center specifically scopes and trains for it. In [manufacturing plants](/industries/manufacturing-plants) we handle offices, restrooms, breakrooms and locker rooms, while production equipment cleaning stays with plant staff who know the machinery and lockout procedures.

Being specific about boundaries protects you. It prevents the gaps that happen when a vendor assumes a task belongs to the client and the client assumes it belongs to the vendor.
