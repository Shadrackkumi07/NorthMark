## What a clean office actually requires

Offices look simple, but they get dirty in predictable, concentrated ways. Most of the soil in an office comes in through the front door and ends up in entry carpets. Most of the germs collect on a short list of touchpoints. Most complaints come from three places: restrooms, breakrooms and the trash.

A good office cleaning program puts effort where it matters:

- **Entries and walk-off areas** catch soil before it spreads. In Fargo–Moorhead that matters even more from November to April, when salt, sand and slush come in on every pair of boots. See [winter facility care in Fargo](/resources/winter-facility-care-fargo).
- **Restrooms** get cleaned and restocked on every visit, because that is where cleanliness is judged first.
- **Breakrooms** get counters, sinks, tables, appliance exteriors and floors cleaned so food residue does not build up.
- **Touchpoints** such as door handles, light switches, elevator buttons and shared equipment get wiped on a schedule. The CDC notes that routine cleaning with soap or detergent removes most germs from surfaces, with disinfection added where there is higher risk.
- **Workspaces** get trash removed, reachable surfaces dusted and floors vacuumed, without moving papers or touching screens.

## How often should an office be cleaned?

It depends on headcount, visitors and how the space is used. As a rule of thumb:

- **Under 15 people, few visitors:** one to two visits a week, with restroom-only visits in between if needed
- **15 to 50 people:** three to five visits a week
- **More than 50 people, or customer-facing:** five visits a week, sometimes with day coverage

Our guide to [how often an office should be cleaned](/resources/how-often-should-an-office-be-cleaned) explains the factors in detail. Small workplaces may also want our [small business janitorial](/services/small-business-janitorial) page.

## Cleaning around confidential work

Offices hold client files, financial records and personal information. Our teams do not read, move or photograph documents or screens, and papers left on desks stay where they are. Desks are wiped only when cleared. If you have rooms that should not be entered, such as file rooms, server closets or executive offices, they can be excluded or cleaned only under conditions you set. See [security and access control](/security-and-access-control).

This matters most for [professional offices](/industries/professional-offices) such as law, accounting and insurance firms, and for [technology and coworking spaces](/industries/technology-coworking) where many tenants share common areas.

## Office cleaning and indoor air quality

Dust and debris affect how an office feels and smells. The EPA's guidance on office indoor air quality points to housekeeping as one of the factors building occupants can influence. Regular vacuuming with well-maintained equipment, dusting of reachable surfaces and prompt trash removal all help. We note visible problems such as water stains, musty odors or blocked vents so building management can address them.
