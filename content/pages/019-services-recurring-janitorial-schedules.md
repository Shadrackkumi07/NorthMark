A recurring janitorial schedule sets how often each part of your building is cleaned: nightly, a few nights a week, weekly or on a split plan where restrooms and trash are done more often than full cleaning. Northmark builds schedules for Fargo, West Fargo and Moorhead buildings around occupancy, traffic and use, then writes them into the scope so every task has a defined frequency. Choosing well is the single biggest factor in both how clean your building feels and what you pay.

## The main schedule options

| Schedule | Typical buildings | Strengths | Trade-offs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Five nights a week | Busy offices, clinics, banks, customer-facing spaces | Consistently clean every morning; restrooms never go a day | Highest cost |
| Three nights a week (e.g., Mon/Wed/Fri) | Moderate-traffic offices of 15–50 people | Good balance of cost and cleanliness | Trash and restrooms can show wear on off days |
| Two nights a week | Small offices, low-traffic professional suites | Affordable; covers essentials | Kitchens and restrooms may need occupant help between visits |
| One night a week | Very small or lightly used spaces | Lowest cost | Noticeable decline before each visit |
| Split schedule | Many buildings of all sizes | Restrooms and trash nightly, full detail less often | Needs a clear written scope to work |
| Daytime plus nightly | Large or high-traffic buildings | Restrooms and lobbies refreshed during the day | Highest coverage and cost |

## Why split schedules often work best

Not every task needs the same frequency. In most offices:

- **Restrooms** get dirty every day and are judged most harshly.
- **Trash** in kitchens and restrooms can smell within a day.
- **Entry floors** collect soil daily, especially in winter.
- **Workstation dusting** and **detail vacuuming** can usually wait several days without anyone noticing.

A split schedule puts nightly effort into the first three and less frequent effort into the rest. For a 30-person office, a split plan with nightly restroom, kitchen, trash and entry service plus twice-weekly full cleaning often costs less than five full nights and feels nearly as clean.

## How we choose a schedule for your building

During the [facility walkthrough](/facility-walkthrough) we look at:

1. **Headcount and visitors.** More people means more soil, more trash and more restroom use.
2. **Restroom-to-occupant ratio.** Fewer restrooms per person means each one needs more frequent attention.
3. **Customer and patient exposure.** Spaces visitors see need higher frequency.
4. **Food.** Breakrooms with heavy use, catered meetings or shared kitchens need nightly attention.
5. **Floor types.** Hard floors show soil faster in some lighting; carpet hides it longer but needs regular vacuuming to last.
6. **Season.** Fargo–Moorhead winters bring salt and slush. Many buildings increase entry frequency from November to April.
7. **Budget.** We show you where reducing frequency is low-risk and where it is not.

You get a recommendation with the reasoning behind it, not just a number. Our guide to [how often an office should be cleaned](/resources/how-often-should-an-office-be-cleaned) explains these factors in more depth.

## Frequency and cost

Labor is most of the cost of janitorial service, and frequency drives labor. Moving from three nights to five does not add exactly two-thirds to the price, because some tasks stay weekly regardless, but it does increase it meaningfully. Our guides to [commercial cleaning cost in Fargo](/resources/commercial-cleaning-cost-fargo) and [janitorial pricing per square foot](/resources/janitorial-pricing-per-square-foot) show how frequency, scope and building size interact.

## Weekly, monthly and quarterly tasks

Every schedule also includes periodic tasks that do not need doing every visit:

- **Weekly:** detail dusting of cleared surfaces, edge vacuuming, partition and wall touch-ups in restrooms, conference room detail
- **Monthly:** high dusting of vents and ledges, baseboards, door frames, interior glass partitions
- **Quarterly or seasonal:** deeper detail of entries after winter, light fixture lenses we can safely reach, and seasonal adjustments

These are written into the scope with specific frequencies, so they actually happen instead of being "as needed."

## Adjusting the schedule over time

Buildings change. Headcount grows, hybrid work schedules change occupancy, a tenant moves out. We review frequency with you periodically and adjust the scope when the building changes. Many Fargo–Moorhead offices with hybrid schedules have moved to concentrating cleaning on the nights after their busiest in-office days.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the most common janitorial schedule?

For busy offices and customer-facing spaces, five nights a week is most common. Moderate-traffic offices often use three nights or a split schedule.

### What is a split janitorial schedule?

A split schedule cleans high-impact areas, such as restrooms, kitchens, trash and entries, more often than lower-impact tasks such as workstation dusting and detail vacuuming.

### Can we change our schedule later?

Yes. We adjust frequency when occupancy, hours or budget change, and update the written scope.

### Do you offer weekly cleaning?

Yes, for small or lightly used spaces. We will tell you honestly if weekly service is likely to leave the space looking worn between visits.
