## Why small businesses struggle with cleaning

Small businesses usually handle cleaning in one of three ways, and each has problems:

1. **Staff do it themselves.** Someone empties trash on Friday, nobody cleans the restroom properly, and the office slowly gets grimy. It also takes paid time away from the work the business exists to do.
2. **A friend-of-a-friend cleaner.** Cheap and personal until they get sick, take a vacation, raise prices or stop showing up, with no insurance and no backup.
3. **A big cleaning company.** Reliable on paper, but small accounts can get the least attention: rotating crews, slow responses and a minimum price built for larger buildings.

Small businesses dominate the local economy. Census County Business Patterns counted about 7,300 employer establishments across Cass and Clay counties in 2023, and the SBA's Office of Advocacy reports that the great majority of U.S. employer firms are small. These businesses need cleaning that is professional and accountable but proportionate.

## How we size janitorial for small businesses

### Right frequency

Most small offices do not need five nights a week. Common plans:

| Business size and type | Typical plan |
|---|---|
| 5–10 people, few visitors | One visit a week, with an optional mid-week restroom and trash visit |
| 10–25 people, some visitors | Two visits a week |
| 25–40 people or customer-facing | Three visits a week, sometimes with restroom-only visits in between |
| Clinics and food-handling businesses | Usually more frequent because of hygiene expectations |

See [recurring janitorial schedules](/services/recurring-janitorial-schedules) and our guide to [how often an office should be cleaned](/resources/how-often-should-an-office-be-cleaned).

### Focused scope

In a small office, a few areas matter most:

- **Restroom:** cleaned thoroughly every visit
- **Kitchen or coffee area:** counters, sink, table, appliance exteriors, floor
- **Trash and recycling:** emptied every visit
- **Entry and reception:** glass, floor, mats and the counter visitors see
- **Floors:** vacuumed and mopped in traffic areas every visit, detailed weekly or monthly

Workstation dusting and detailed vacuuming can rotate through the month. Our [office cleaning checklist](/resources/office-cleaning-checklist) shows how to split tasks.

### No big-building overhead

We do not force small accounts into a large-building pricing model. The price follows the labor your space actually needs, based on a walkthrough.

## The same standards as large accounts

Size does not change the basics:

- **Background-checked, assigned staff:** the same people each visit
- **Insurance and bonding**, with certificates available to your landlord
- **Logged keys and codes**, with lock-up checks every visit
- **Written scope**, so everyone knows what is included
- **Supervisor QA walkthroughs**, so quality is checked, not assumed
- **One contact**, who answers when you call

## Keeping a small office clean between visits

A few habits stretch the effect of weekly or twice-weekly service:

- Put lidded bins in the kitchen and restroom and empty food waste daily
- Keep a basic supply of disinfecting wipes for shared equipment
- Ask staff to clear desks before cleaning nights
- Place good walk-off mats at the entry, especially in winter
- Tell your cleaner about busy weeks or events so a visit can be added

## Is outsourcing worth it for a small business?

Compare the hours staff spend cleaning, at their pay rate, with a professional quote, then add the value of a cleaner building. Our guide to [in-house vs outsourced janitorial](/resources/in-house-vs-outsourced-janitorial) walks through the math. For most small offices, a professional who cleans once or twice a week costs less than the staff time it replaces, and does a better job.
