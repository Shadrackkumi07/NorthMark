Janitorial service management is the work of running a cleaning account so the client does not have to: owning communication, tracking and closing issues, adjusting scope and staffing, and reporting results. At Northmark every account has a named account lead who is responsible for your building's service, reachable directly, and accountable for fixing problems and preventing repeats. It is the part of janitorial service most buyers never see in a proposal, and the part that most often decides whether the relationship works.

## Why cleaning accounts fail without management

Ask facility buyers why they changed cleaning vendors and the answers rarely involve how well the crew used a mop. They involve management:

- Calls and emails disappeared into a general inbox
- The same problem was reported three times
- Nobody noticed a crew change until things got worse
- Scope disagreements dragged on
- Nobody from the company visited after the contract was signed

Industry management standards, such as ISSA's Cleaning Industry Management Standard, emphasize documented processes for service delivery, quality, training and customer communication for exactly this reason. Management systems, more than effort, separate consistent providers from inconsistent ones.

## What your account lead does

### Owns communication

You have one person to call or email. They answer, or respond promptly, and they know your building, scope and history. If they are away, a named backup covers.

### Logs and closes issues

Every issue you report is logged with the date, description and location. It stays open until the fix is confirmed with you. Repeated issues are flagged for root-cause review.

### Watches quality

The account lead reviews supervisor QA results for your building, follows up on deficiencies and spots trends before you do. See our [quality assurance program](/quality-assurance-program).

### Manages staffing

Assigned team members, training on your site's specifics, coverage for absences, and advance notice of any assignment change are all the account lead's responsibility.

### Keeps the scope current

When your building changes, such as new tenants, hybrid schedules, a remodel or added space, the account lead updates the scope and checklist so the service follows.

### Reports

You receive the information you need, at a level of detail that fits: QA results, open issues, upcoming periodic tasks and recommendations.

## The issue lifecycle

| Step | What happens | Who owns it |
|---|---|---|
| Report | You report an issue by phone or email | You |
| Log | Issue recorded with location and details | Account lead |
| Assign | Corrective action assigned to the team | Account lead |
| Correct | Issue fixed, ideally on the next visit | Cleaning team |
| Verify | Supervisor or lead confirms the fix | Supervisor |
| Confirm | You are told it is resolved | Account lead |
| Review | Repeated issues trigger a cause review | Account lead and supervisor |

That lifecycle is simple, but it is the difference between reporting something once and reporting it every week.

## Periodic reviews

Beyond day-to-day issues, we review each account with the client periodically, often quarterly for larger accounts. A review covers:

- QA score trends by area
- Issues reported and resolved
- Scope fit: are frequencies still right for how the building is used?
- Upcoming seasonal changes, such as the Fargo winter entry program
- Any changes in security procedures or contacts

## When management means telling you something unwelcome

Good account management includes honest conversations. If a recurring problem is caused by a scope that does not allow enough time, we will say so rather than quietly cutting corners. If building conditions, such as a broken dispenser, a leaking fixture or poor matting, are making cleaning harder, we will tell you and suggest a fix. Accountability works in both directions.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who do we call when something goes wrong?

Your named Northmark account lead, by phone or email. If they are unavailable, a named backup covers.

### How do we know an issue has been fixed?

Issues stay open until a supervisor or the account lead verifies the fix and confirms it with you.

### Do you hold regular account reviews?

Yes. Larger accounts typically have quarterly reviews covering QA trends, issues, scope fit and seasonal changes.

### Will you tell us if our scope needs more time?

Yes. If repeated problems come from a scope that does not allow enough labor, we explain that and propose options.
