Most facility vendors only see a building from the ground. Most drone operators only see it from the air, once. Northmark sits in the middle. We clean and maintain buildings, so we understand what an owner actually needs to know, and we document them from above so that information can be seen.

That combination is unusual in the Fargo–Moorhead market. A property manager who already trusts Northmark with janitorial or maintenance work can add roof and site documentation under the same relationship, with the same written scope and the same point of contact.

## How we fly: Part 107 and local airspace

Commercial drone work in the United States is governed by the FAA's Part 107 rules. Among other requirements, they call for a certificated remote pilot, registered aircraft, flights generally at or below 400 feet above ground level, visual line of sight, and authorization before flying in controlled airspace. FAA rules also require Remote ID broadcast for most registered drones.

Fargo has controlled airspace around Hector International Airport, and Moorhead has its own municipal airport. Many commercial properties in the metro sit inside or near controlled airspace. We check every site before the mission and obtain FAA authorization, typically through the LAANC system, where it is required. If a site cannot be flown legally or safely, we tell you before scheduling rather than after.

## Our process

1. **Scoping call or walkthrough.** We confirm the property, what you need documented, the deliverable format and your deadline.
2. **Airspace and site check.** We review airspace, obstacles, nearby people and roads, and obtain any required authorization.
3. **Flight.** The mission is flown by our Part 107 remote pilot under the conditions of the rules and any authorization.
4. **Processing.** Images are organized by area and labeled with date and location.
5. **Delivery.** You receive the imagery and a summary of visible observations. Where a specialist assessment is needed, such as a roofer, engineer or adjuster, we say so.

## What aerial documentation is, and is not

Aerial imagery shows visible conditions. It is very good at revealing ponding water, debris, damaged membranes, missing flashing, displaced equipment, cracked paving and changes over time. It does not replace a structural engineer, a roofing contractor's hands-on assessment or an insurance adjuster's determination. We document. Licensed professionals diagnose and repair. Our guide to [drone vs manual roof inspection](/resources/drone-vs-manual-roof-inspection) covers when each approach makes sense.

## Building a condition record over time

The most valuable aerial imagery is often the "before" picture. A roof documented every spring and fall, or before each hail season, gives you a baseline to compare against after a storm. That baseline can make claims, warranty discussions and capital planning much more straightforward. Many clients pair aerial missions with our [property condition reports](/services/property-condition-reports) for a complete record.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is your drone pilot FAA-authorized?

Yes. Northmark's aerial missions are flown by a remote pilot holding an FAA Part 107 remote pilot certificate.

### Can you fly near Hector International Airport?

Many sites near the airport are in controlled airspace. We check each site and obtain FAA authorization, typically through LAANC, where it is required. Some locations may have altitude limits or need additional approval.

### Do you provide a roof condition diagnosis?

We provide imagery and a summary of visible observations. Diagnosis and repair recommendations should come from a roofing contractor or engineer, who can use our imagery.

### How quickly can you document storm damage?

We schedule post-storm missions as soon as weather and airspace allow. Imagery is dated so it can support claims and contractor bids.
