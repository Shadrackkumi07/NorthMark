Northmark documents the condition of commercial properties at the start and end of a lease, or at key points in between, using drone imagery of the roof, exterior and site plus ground-level photos of interior common areas and spaces the parties designate. Landlords, tenants and property managers in Fargo, West Fargo and Moorhead use the dated record to reduce disputes over damage, wear and restoration obligations when a lease ends.

## Why lease condition documentation matters

Commercial leases often require tenants to return space in a specified condition, allowing for reasonable wear, and many make tenants responsible for certain repairs during the term. When the lease ends, the parties have to agree on what changed. Without a record of the starting point, that conversation relies on memory, scattered photos and goodwill.

The SBA's guidance on choosing a business location encourages tenants to understand their lease responsibilities before signing. A neutral, dated record of condition at signing is one of the most practical ways for both sides to protect themselves.

Disputes are especially common over areas nobody looks at often:

- **Roofs**, where single-tenant leases may assign maintenance to the tenant
- **Exterior walls and signage**, where tenant signs are installed and removed
- **Parking lots and truck courts**, where heavy tenant use may cause damage
- **Dock areas, yards and outdoor storage** at industrial properties

## What we document

| Area | Method | What it captures |
|---|---|---|
| Roof | Drone | Surface condition, drains, penetrations, rooftop units, tenant-installed equipment |
| Exterior walls | Drone and ground | Cladding, masonry, windows, sealants, signage and mounting points |
| Site and parking | Drone | Paving, striping, curbs, drainage, fencing, dumpster areas, truck courts |
| Interior common areas | Ground photos | Entries, corridors, restrooms and areas the parties designate |
| Tenant space (optional) | Ground photos | Floors, walls, ceilings and fixtures as agreed by both parties |

Imagery is dated, organized by area and delivered to the party who ordered it, or to both parties if the lease calls for a joint record.

## When to document

- **Lease start:** before the tenant takes possession or begins improvements
- **Major alterations:** before and after tenant improvements, signage or rooftop installations
- **Lease end:** after the tenant vacates and before restoration negotiations
- **Renewal:** at renewal, to reset the baseline

## A neutral record

We do not interpret lease language, decide who is responsible for what or estimate restoration costs. Those questions belong to the parties, their attorneys and contractors. Our role is to record visible condition clearly and consistently, so the parties argue less about facts and more productively about obligations. Many landlords and tenants agree in advance to share the same documentation.

## Industrial and single-tenant properties

Single-tenant industrial and retail leases are where documentation pays off most. Tenants often control the whole building for years, and roofs, paving and docks change significantly. A start-of-lease aerial record of the roof and site, paired with a [parking lot and site condition survey](/services/parking-lot-site-condition-survey), gives both sides a clear baseline.

## Multifamily and multi-tenant properties

For [property managers](/industries/property-management) overseeing apartments and multi-tenant buildings, documenting common areas and exteriors at owner turnover or management transitions serves a similar purpose. See [multifamily common areas](/industries/multifamily-common-areas).

## Part of a broader condition record

Lease documentation fits naturally into ongoing [property condition reports](/services/property-condition-reports) and periodic [drone roof inspections](/services/drone-roof-inspection). Owners who document regularly already have most of what they need when a lease turns over.

## What makes documentation useful in a dispute

Condition records are most persuasive when they are dated, complete and consistent. Photos taken on a phone at different times, from different angles and with no record of who took them are easy to question. Our records are captured in a single session, organized by area, labeled with the date and taken from repeatable positions, so the end-of-lease set can be compared directly with the start-of-lease set. Both parties can see the same evidence, which tends to shorten negotiations and keep them focused on what the lease actually says.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who orders lease condition documentation?

Landlords, tenants or property managers, and sometimes both parties jointly. We deliver imagery to whoever orders it, or to both parties if agreed.

### Do you document inside the tenant's space?

Optionally, if both parties agree. Our standard package covers the roof, exterior, site and common areas.

### Will you decide who is responsible for damage?

No. We record visible condition. Responsibility under the lease is for the parties and their advisors to determine.

### When should documentation happen?

At lease start before possession, before and after major alterations, at lease end after move-out, and optionally at renewal.
