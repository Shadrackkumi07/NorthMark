Northmark provides routine janitorial service for medical offices and outpatient clinics in Fargo, West Fargo and Moorhead. We clean waiting rooms, reception, restrooms, offices, corridors, breakrooms and the non-clinical surfaces of exam rooms the practice designates, using background-checked staff trained on privacy and on the boundaries between janitorial work and clinical responsibilities. Every practice gets a written scope and supervisor QA walkthroughs.

## Why medical offices need a different cleaning scope

A medical office is judged by patients the moment they walk in. A dusty waiting room or a neglected restroom undermines confidence in the care that follows. Medical offices also carry obligations an ordinary office does not:

- **Infection prevention expectations.** The CDC's environmental infection control guidelines emphasize routine cleaning of environmental surfaces, with particular attention to frequently touched surfaces in patient-care areas.
- **Regulated waste and sharps.** OSHA's bloodborne pathogens standard governs how employers handle potentially infectious materials. Janitorial teams must know what they should never touch.
- **Patient privacy.** Under the HIPAA Privacy Rule, practices must reasonably safeguard protected health information. Cleaning staff are present after hours when records and screens may be accessible.

The Fargo area also has an unusually large healthcare sector. BLS data show healthcare practitioner and technical occupations make up 8.5% of local employment, against 6.3% nationally. That means a large number of clinics, specialty practices and outpatient centers, each needing reliable cleaning.

## Clear boundaries with clinical staff

The most important part of a medical office cleaning scope is often what it leaves out. During onboarding we agree in writing who handles what:

- **Regulated medical waste and sharps containers** are handled by the practice and its licensed waste hauler, not by janitorial staff. Our team does not open, move or empty sharps containers or red-bag waste.
- **Exam tables, medical equipment and instruments** are cleaned and disinfected by clinical staff between patients, using the products and dwell times your infection prevention program requires.
- **Spills of blood or body fluids** are handled under the practice's exposure control plan unless we have specifically scoped and trained for it.
- **Disinfectant products** used by our team in patient areas are agreed with the practice, so they are compatible with your surfaces and protocols.

These boundaries protect patients, protect your staff and protect our team. Our guide to [OSHA bloodborne pathogens and medical office cleaning](/resources/osha-bloodborne-pathogens-medical-office-cleaning) explains why they matter.

## Privacy and security

Medical offices hold patient records, prescription pads and sometimes controlled substances. Our cleaning staff are background-checked, assigned to specific accounts and trained not to read, move or photograph documents or screens. Rooms the practice designates as restricted, such as medication storage or records rooms, are excluded or cleaned only under the conditions you set. See [security and access control](/security-and-access-control).

## Cleaning vs disinfecting in a clinic

Many practices ask whether their janitorial service "disinfects." The honest answer depends on the surface and product. Cleaning removes soil, sanitizing reduces germs and disinfecting kills specific organisms when an EPA-registered product is used according to its label, including contact time. Our guide to [cleaning vs sanitizing vs disinfecting](/resources/cleaning-vs-sanitizing-vs-disinfecting) covers the definitions. We agree with each practice which surfaces get which treatment.

## Scheduling

Most medical offices are cleaned after the last patient leaves. Practices with evening clinics or weekend hours get schedules that fit around them. Larger practices may also need light daytime attention to restrooms and waiting areas.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do you clean exam rooms?

Yes, the surfaces the practice designates: floors, non-regulated trash, cleared counters, sinks and touchpoints. Exam tables, equipment and instruments are cleaned by clinical staff between patients.

### Do your cleaners handle sharps or medical waste?

No. Sharps containers and regulated medical waste are handled by the practice and its waste hauler.

### Are your staff trained on patient privacy?

Yes. Staff are background-checked and trained not to read, move or photograph documents or screens. Restricted rooms are excluded or cleaned under the practice's conditions.

### Can you use the disinfectants our practice specifies?

Yes. We agree products for patient areas with the practice so they fit your surfaces and infection prevention protocols.
