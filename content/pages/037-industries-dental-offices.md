Northmark provides routine janitorial service for dental practices in Fargo, West Fargo and Moorhead. We clean reception and waiting areas, restrooms, business offices, staff lounges, corridors and the floors and non-clinical surfaces of operatories the practice designates. Dental unit surfaces, instruments, sterilization equipment and clinical contact surfaces remain with your clinical team, as CDC dental infection control guidance assigns them. Our staff are background-checked and trained on patient privacy.

## What makes dental offices different

Dental practices look like small medical offices, but their cleaning needs are distinct:

- **Aerosols and splatter.** Dental procedures generate spray that settles on surfaces in and around operatories. Floors and walls near treatment areas need consistent attention.
- **Strict clinical protocols.** The CDC's guidance for dental settings sets out how clinical contact surfaces, dental unit waterlines, instruments and sterilization are handled. That work belongs to trained dental staff.
- **Patient anxiety.** Many patients arrive nervous. A clean, calm, fresh-smelling reception area and restroom matter more than in most businesses.
- **Many small rooms.** Operatories, a sterilization area, an imaging room, a lab, a business office and a lounge all need different treatment.

## How we divide responsibilities

The key to cleaning a dental office well is a written split between janitorial and clinical work. A typical division looks like this:

| Area or item | Northmark janitorial team | Dental clinical team |
|---|---|---|
| Reception, waiting room, business office | Full cleaning | — |
| Restrooms | Full cleaning and supply reporting | — |
| Staff lounge and kitchen | Full cleaning | — |
| Operatory floors | Mopping with agreed product | — |
| Operatory walls, door handles, light switches | Cleaning as scoped | — |
| Dental chair, delivery unit, light handles, clinical contact surfaces | — | Cleaning and disinfection between patients |
| Instruments and sterilization equipment | — | Processing per practice protocol |
| Dental unit waterlines | — | Maintenance per manufacturer and CDC guidance |
| Sharps and regulated waste | — | Handling by practice and licensed hauler |
| Sterilization room counters | Only if the practice designates specific surfaces | Clinical surfaces and equipment |

We write this division into your scope during onboarding, so there are no gaps and no overlaps.

## Floors carry most of the janitorial workload

In dental offices, floors do a lot of work. Operatory floors catch settled spray, and corridor floors carry traffic between rooms all day. Most dental floors are resilient tile or sheet vinyl that responds well to regular damp mopping with the right product. We agree products with the practice, so they are compatible with your flooring and do not conflict with clinical disinfectants.

## Privacy and security

Dental practices hold patient records, insurance information and sometimes controlled substances. OSHA's dentistry resources also remind practices of chemical and sharps hazards that cleaning staff need to know about. Our approach:

- Background-checked staff assigned to your practice ([background-checked staff](/background-checked-staff))
- No reading, moving or photographing of documents, screens or charts
- Restricted rooms, such as drug storage and records, excluded or cleaned only under your conditions
- Safety data sheets for our products available on site, and awareness of hazards in your practice

## Scheduling

Most dental offices are cleaned in the evening after the last patient and after clinical staff finish their close-down routine. Practices with early-morning hygiene appointments may prefer early-morning cleaning. Practices open on Saturdays get a schedule that fits. ## Questions to settle during onboarding

Every practice runs a little differently, so we confirm a few specifics before the first night: which operatory surfaces your team wipes at close and which we touch, where our products are stored so they never mix with clinical supplies, whether the lab and imaging rooms are in scope, how the sterilization room door is handled after hours, and who to call if we find equipment left running or a water line leaking. Writing those answers down once prevents months of small misunderstandings.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do you clean dental operatories?

We clean operatory floors and the walls, doors and switches the practice designates. Dental chairs, delivery units and clinical contact surfaces are cleaned and disinfected by your clinical team between patients.

### Do you handle sharps or sterilization?

No. Sharps, regulated waste, instrument processing and sterilization stay with your clinical team and licensed waste hauler.

### What products do you use on operatory floors?

Products are agreed with each practice so they are compatible with your flooring and infection control program.

### Can you clean on Saturdays?

Yes. Practices with Saturday hours can schedule cleaning after close or on another day that suits them.
