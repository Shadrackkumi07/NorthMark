Northmark provides janitorial support for ambulatory surgical centers in Fargo, West Fargo and Moorhead, focused on the non-sterile areas: waiting rooms, reception, business offices, staff lounges and locker rooms, public restrooms, corridors outside restricted zones and administrative spaces. Operating rooms, procedure rooms, sterile processing and other restricted clinical areas remain with the center's trained environmental services staff, whose terminal cleaning procedures are part of its infection control program. We put that boundary in writing before the first visit.

## Why boundaries come first in a surgical center

Surgical centers operate under strict infection control expectations. Medicare-participating ambulatory surgical centers must meet conditions for coverage, including maintaining a sanitary environment and an infection control program, as set out in 42 CFR Part 416. The CDC's environmental infection control guidelines describe how cleaning and disinfection practices are matched to the risk of each area.

Operating room turnover and terminal cleaning, sterile processing and cleaning in semi-restricted and restricted zones require specific training, products, dwell times, attire and documentation under the center's own program. A general janitorial vendor should not drift into that work.

What a surgical center does need from a janitorial partner is dependable, high-standard cleaning of everything else, so its clinical environmental services staff can concentrate on the clinical zones.

## A typical division of responsibility

| Area | Northmark janitorial team | Center clinical/EVS staff |
|---|---|---|
| Entrance, waiting room, reception | Full cleaning | — |
| Public restrooms | Full cleaning and supply reporting | — |
| Business and administrative offices | Full cleaning | — |
| Staff lounge, kitchen, locker rooms | Full cleaning | — |
| Corridors in unrestricted areas | Floors, walls, touchpoints | — |
| Pre-op and recovery bays | Only surfaces the center designates in writing, if any | Clinical surfaces and equipment |
| Operating and procedure rooms | — | Turnover and terminal cleaning |
| Sterile processing and storage | — | All cleaning |
| Regulated waste and sharps | — | Handling with licensed hauler |

Every center's layout and program differ. We work from your infection prevention lead's direction and document the final division in your scope.

## Standards we hold in the areas we clean

- **Products agreed with the center.** Disinfectants and cleaners used in any patient-facing area are approved by your infection prevention lead so they fit your protocols and surfaces.
- **Clear zone rules.** Our team knows which doors they do not pass through, and attire requirements for any area they enter.
- **Bloodborne pathogen awareness.** OSHA's bloodborne pathogens standard governs occupational exposure. Our team does not handle regulated waste or sharps and follows the center's procedure if they encounter potentially infectious material.
- **Documentation.** Visit logs and checklist completion are available for your records and surveys.
- **Supervisor QA.** Recurring inspections scored against the scope. See our [quality assurance program](/quality-assurance-program).

## Patient experience matters too

Patients arrive at a surgical center anxious and leave groggy. The waiting room, restrooms and discharge areas they and their families use shape how they perceive the center's care. Consistent, careful cleaning of those spaces is a direct contribution to patient satisfaction.

## Privacy and access

Surgical centers hold patient records, medications and expensive equipment. Our team is background-checked and assigned to your center, and is trained not to read, move or photograph records or screens. Medication rooms and restricted areas stay off-limits unless your team specifies otherwise. See [security and access control](/security-and-access-control).

## Scheduling

Most centers finish cases by mid-afternoon or early evening. We schedule after clinical close-down so we never interfere with turnover or EVS work, and coordinate with your EVS lead so both teams know when the other is in the building.

## Coordinating with your EVS team

The janitorial team and the center's environmental services staff share a building and should never get in each other's way. We agree arrival times so our work begins after clinical turnover is complete, keep our carts and products in a designated location separate from clinical supplies, and use a short handoff note if either team notices something the other should know, such as a leaking fixture in a corridor or a door that is not latching. Clear coordination prevents duplicated effort and missed areas.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do you clean operating rooms?

No. Operating rooms, procedure rooms and sterile processing remain with the center's trained environmental services staff. We clean non-sterile areas such as waiting rooms, offices, restrooms, staff areas and unrestricted corridors.

### Can you use the products our infection prevention team specifies?

Yes. Products used in any patient-facing area are approved by your infection prevention lead.

### Do you handle regulated medical waste?

No. Regulated waste and sharps are handled by the center and its licensed hauler.

### Can you provide documentation for surveys?

Yes. Visit logs, checklists and supervisor inspection records are available for your files.
