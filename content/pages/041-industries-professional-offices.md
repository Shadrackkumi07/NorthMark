## The confidentiality problem in professional offices

Professional firms carry formal duties to protect client information:

- **Lawyers** are bound by confidentiality rules. The American Bar Association's Model Rule 1.6 requires lawyers to make reasonable efforts to prevent unauthorized access to client information.
- **Tax preparers and accountants** are expected to safeguard taxpayer data. IRS Publication 4557 includes physical security among the steps firms should take.
- **Insurance agencies and financial advisors** handle personal and financial information covered by federal and state privacy expectations. The FTC's guide to protecting personal information recommends limiting physical access to sensitive records.

Each of those duties extends to vendors with building access. A cleaning crew working alone in your office at night walks past files, inboxes, unlocked screens and printed client documents. A reasonable firm should know who those people are and what they are allowed to do.

## What we clean

- **Kitchens and coffee areas**, often heavily used in busy seasons

## Seasonal workloads

Professional offices have busy seasons. Accounting firms run long hours from January to April and again before extension deadlines. Law firms surge around trials and closings. During those periods kitchens, restrooms and trash fill faster, and staff are often in the office during our normal cleaning window. We adjust: more frequent attention to kitchens and restrooms in busy season, and working around late staff without disturbing them.

## What a confidentiality-first visit looks like

On a typical evening in a law or accounting office, our assigned cleaner arrives after the last staff member leaves, disarms the alarm with an individual code and works through the scope in a fixed order. Private offices are entered only to empty trash, vacuum and wipe surfaces that are already clear. Anything left on a desk, chair or printer tray stays exactly where it was. Conference rooms are reset for the morning, kitchens and restrooms cleaned, and entries checked. Before leaving, the cleaner walks the lock-up list, re-arms the alarm and logs the visit. If a partner's office door is locked, it stays locked, and the cleaner notes it rather than looking for a key. That predictability is what professional firms are buying.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will your cleaners look at or move our documents?

No. Documents, files and mail stay exactly where they are. Desks are wiped only when cleared, and staff never read or photograph papers or screens.

### Can certain offices or file rooms be off-limits?

Yes. Restricted rooms can be excluded entirely or cleaned only under conditions you set, for example during business hours with a staff member present.

### Do you empty shred bins?

No. Locked shred consoles and secure document bins are handled by your shredding vendor.

### Can you adjust service during tax season?

Yes. We increase attention to kitchens, restrooms and trash during busy seasons and work around staff who stay late.
