Northmark cleans the common areas of apartment and multifamily properties in Fargo, West Fargo and Moorhead: entries and lobbies, mailrooms, hallways, stairwells, elevators, laundry rooms, fitness centers, community rooms and trash rooms. Each property gets a written scope and schedule, background-checked staff, and supervisor inspections reported to the property manager, so the parts of a building every resident shares stay consistently clean.

## Why common areas drive resident satisfaction

Residents may never see your maintenance shop or your books, but they walk through your lobby, hallways and mailroom every day. Those spaces shape how residents feel about the property and how prospects judge it on a tour. Dirty carpet in a corridor, a smelly trash room or a dusty fitness center becomes a review, a complaint or a non-renewal.

The Fargo–Moorhead metro has a large renter population, with many young professionals, students and new arrivals, and has added significant multifamily housing as it has grown. Competition for residents makes common-area condition a real business issue.

Frequency depends on the number of units, traffic and amenities. A 24-unit building and a 250-unit complex need very different schedules. We set them during a [facility walkthrough](/facility-walkthrough).

## Winter is the hardest season

Multifamily entries in Fargo–Moorhead take enormous winter traffic: residents, guests and delivery drivers all tracking in snow, slush and ice melt. Salt residue spreads through hallways and into elevators. Our winter scopes add entry and elevator attention, mat management and corridor checks. See [winter facility care in Fargo](/resources/winter-facility-care-fargo).

## Trash rooms and pests

Trash rooms, chute rooms and laundry areas are where pest and odor problems start. The EPA's guidance on integrated pest management in multifamily housing emphasizes sanitation and removing food sources as part of prevention. Regular cleaning of these spaces, plus prompt reporting of overflow, damaged doors or pest signs, supports whatever pest program the property runs.

## Security in residential buildings

Our staff work in buildings where people live. Background-checked, assigned team members, controlled credentials and clear rules about resident interaction are essential:

- Staff wear identification and know who to call with resident concerns
- Credentials to common areas, mechanical rooms and amenity spaces are logged
- Staff do not enter resident units
- Doors that do not latch or secure entries that fail are reported immediately

See [security and access control](/security-and-access-control).

## Reporting to the property manager

Supervisors inspect common areas on a recurring schedule and report findings to the property manager, including maintenance issues our team notices: burned-out lights, damaged carpet, leaking fixtures, broken entry hardware. Combined with our [commercial property maintenance](/services/commercial-property-maintenance) service and [property condition reports](/services/property-condition-reports), that gives managers a clear picture of each property between their own visits.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do you clean inside apartment units?

No. Northmark cleans common areas only. We do not enter or clean resident units.

### How often should apartment hallways be cleaned?

Most properties vacuum hallways weekly to several times a week, with lobbies, mailrooms and elevators cleaned more often. Frequency depends on unit count and traffic.

### Do you clean fitness centers and clubhouses?

Yes. Amenity spaces can be included in the scope, along with cleaning after scheduled community events.

### Will you report maintenance problems you find?

Yes. Our team reports lights out, damaged fixtures, leaks and entry hardware problems to the property manager.
