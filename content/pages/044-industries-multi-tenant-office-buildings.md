## The challenge of a shared building

A multi-tenant office building is several businesses under one roof, each with its own hours, expectations and sensitivities, plus common areas everyone shares. Cleaning problems in these buildings usually come from fragmentation:

- Management hires one vendor for common areas while tenants hire others for suites, so three or four crews hold keys to the same building.
- Standards vary between floors, and the lobby looks great while a tenant's corridor does not.
- Nobody owns the gaps. Who cleans the shared kitchen on floor three? Who handles the conference center?
- Complaints go to the property manager, who has to sort out which vendor is responsible.

## Common-area standards

Common areas set the tone for the whole building. Typical nightly tasks include:

- Lobby glass, entry floors, mats, directories and reception surfaces
- Elevator floors, walls, panels and door tracks
- Common restroom cleaning, fixture disinfection, floor care and supply reporting
- Corridor vacuuming and spot cleaning
- Trash and recycling removal from common receptacles
- Stairwell sweeping and handrail cleaning on a weekly cycle

Industry groups such as BOMA International publish standards and guidance that many office owners use to benchmark operations. We write a building-wide scope that reflects your class of building and tenant expectations, and inspect against it. See our [quality assurance program](/quality-assurance-program).

## Tenant suites

Tenants vary. A law firm needs strict confidentiality ([professional offices](/industries/professional-offices)), a clinic needs clinical boundaries ([medical offices](/industries/medical-offices)), and a tech startup may want a late cleaning window because staff work into the evening ([technology and coworking](/industries/technology-coworking)). We write each suite's scope separately, honor its restricted rooms and assign consistent staff.

For property managers, offering suite cleaning through the building's vendor can be an amenity: tenants get a vetted, insured vendor already on site, and management keeps control of who has keys.

That catches problems before tenants report them.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you clean both common areas and tenant suites?

Yes. We clean common areas for management and can clean tenant suites under the same program, billed through management or directly to tenants.

### How do you handle keys for many tenants?

Credentials are logged by floor and suite, assigned to specific team members, and each tenant's restricted rooms are honored.

### Do you report inspection results by floor?

Yes. Supervisor inspections can be reported by floor and suite, so management sees exactly where attention is needed.

### Can tenants contract with you directly?

Yes. Tenants can contract suite cleaning directly while management contracts common areas.
