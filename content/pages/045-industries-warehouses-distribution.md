## Why warehouse cleaning is its own discipline

Warehouses combine two very different buildings: an office and an industrial floor. The office side looks like any workplace, but it usually gets more dust and debris from the floor next door. The floor side has forklifts, pallets, dock doors, racking and people working against shipping deadlines.

## Working safely around operations

OSHA's warehousing guidance highlights forklifts, docks, material storage and slips, trips and falls among common hazards. OSHA's walking-working surfaces standard requires floors to be kept clean and, as far as practical, free of hazards. Our janitorial work supports that, while your safety program stays in charge.

On warehouse accounts our teams:

- Follow your PPE rules (high-visibility vests, safety shoes, eye protection where required)
- Use marked pedestrian walkways and stay out of active equipment zones
- Schedule floor work when traffic is lowest
- Sign wet floors and keep cords and equipment clear of walkways
- Report spills, leaks, damaged dock plates or blocked exits to your designated contact

## Scheduling around shifts

Many distribution centers run two or three shifts, so there is no truly empty building. We schedule by zone:

- **Office wing:** after administrative hours
- **Restrooms and breakrooms:** between shift changes, when they are least used
- **Floor areas:** during the lowest-traffic window you identify
- **Driver areas:** around dock schedules

The schedule is written into the scope, and supervisors check it during QA walkthroughs.

## Restrooms carry the heaviest load

In a warehouse with 80 people per shift, restrooms and breakrooms take far more use than in an office of the same size. They are also where morale problems show first. We usually recommend higher frequency in these areas than in the office wing and track them closely during supervisor inspections.
