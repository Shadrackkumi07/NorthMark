Northmark provides janitorial service for churches and places of worship in Fargo, West Fargo and Moorhead. We clean sanctuaries, narthexes and entries, fellowship halls, classrooms, nurseries, offices, kitchens' non-food-prep areas and restrooms on schedules built around services, weddings, funerals and community events. Our team is background-checked, works from a written scope agreed with church leadership and treats sacred spaces and older finishes with care.

## Churches are busy, irregular buildings

A church building may be quiet on Tuesday morning and packed on Sunday, then host a funeral Wednesday, a youth group Thursday night and a wedding Saturday. Many also run preschools, food pantries, recovery meetings and community programs during the week. That irregular rhythm is the main challenge: a fixed nightly schedule wastes money on quiet days and falls short after big events.

The Fargo–Moorhead area has a strong community of congregations, many in older buildings with stained glass, wood pews, plaster and masonry, and others in newer multipurpose facilities on the edges of town.

## Scheduling around the church calendar

| Event | Typical cleaning approach |
|---|---|
| Weekend services | Full cleaning before the weekend and a reset after services |
| Weddings and funerals | Pre-event touch-up and post-event cleaning of sanctuary, fellowship hall and restrooms |
| Weekday programs | Classrooms, restrooms and meeting rooms after use |
| Holiday seasons | Added visits around Christmas, Easter and other high-attendance services |
| Quiet weeks | Lighter visits focused on restrooms, entries and offices |

We review the church calendar with your administrator or facilities volunteer and adjust visits accordingly. See [recurring janitorial schedules](/services/recurring-janitorial-schedules).

## What we clean

- **Sanctuary:** pews and chairs dusted, floors vacuumed or mopped, hymnal racks emptied of trash, entry glass, chancel or altar area only as church leadership specifies
- **Narthex and entries:** floors, mats, doors, glass, coat areas
- **Fellowship hall:** tables and chairs after events, floors, trash
- **Classrooms and youth rooms:** tables, floors, trash, touchpoints
- **Nurseries:** floors, surfaces and restrooms with products agreed with the church; toy sanitizing as scoped
- **Offices:** trash, surfaces, floors
- **Restrooms:** full cleaning every visit
- **Kitchen:** floors, counters after volunteers finish, trash. Food preparation surfaces stay with the people who prepare food

## Care for sacred and historic spaces

Altars, sacred objects, vestments and liturgical items are handled only as leadership directs, and often not at all. Older churches have finishes that need gentle treatment: wood pews and woodwork, stained and leaded glass, plaster and historic masonry. The National Park Service's preservation guidance warns that harsh cleaning methods can damage historic masonry. We use mild methods, test where unsure and ask before touching anything unusual. Restorative work on historic materials belongs to conservation specialists.

## Nurseries and children's areas

Nurseries and children's ministry rooms need the same attention as a childcare center. Floors are the priority, touchpoints are cleaned, and products are agreed with the church. If your church runs a licensed preschool or daycare, see [childcare centers](/industries/childcare-centers).

## Trust and screening

Churches welcome everyone, but the people who hold keys to the building after hours should be screened. Every Northmark team member is background-checked before receiving access, credentials are logged, and lock-up is verified every visit. Churches with child-protection policies that require specific screening can specify it. See [background-checked staff](/background-checked-staff).

## Budget-conscious service

Most congregations work within tight budgets, and many have relied on volunteers. A professional scope focused on what volunteers struggle to keep up with, such as restrooms, floors and post-event cleanup, can complement volunteer efforts rather than replace them entirely.

## Building upkeep

Older church buildings benefit from regular condition checks: roofs, gutters, entries, basements in spring and heating-season drafts. Our [commercial property maintenance](/services/commercial-property-maintenance) service adds scheduled walkthroughs and reports. Winter entries are another priority; see [winter facility care in Fargo](/resources/winter-facility-care-fargo).

## Working with volunteers and staff

Many churches have a facilities committee, sexton or volunteer team. We coordinate with them, agree who handles which tasks, and leave a short note after each visit about anything that needs attention, such as a broken fixture or a door that will not latch, so nothing falls between paid and volunteer help.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you clean after Sunday services?

Yes. Many churches schedule a reset after weekend services plus a full cleaning before the next weekend.

### Do you clean after weddings and funerals?

Yes. Event cleaning can be added as needed, including pre-event touch-ups.

### How do you handle sacred items?

Altars, sacred objects and liturgical items are handled only as church leadership directs, and often left untouched.

Yes. We serve churches in Moorhead, Fargo and West Fargo.
