Northmark provides janitorial service for technology companies, software firms, startups and coworking spaces in Fargo, West Fargo and Moorhead. We clean open-plan workspaces, phone booths, shared kitchens, lounges, conference rooms and restrooms around flexible and late hours, never touch hardware or screens, honor restricted server and lab rooms, and use background-checked staff with logged credentials, which matters in buildings full of equipment and data.

## Fargo's technology sector

Fargo has a long-established technology presence, including major software operations and a growing startup and AgTech scene, and Greater Fargo Moorhead Economic Development lists technology among the region's target industries. Downtown Fargo in particular has become home to tech companies and coworking spaces in renovated buildings. These workplaces look and operate differently from traditional offices, and their cleaning needs follow.

## What makes tech offices different

- **Open plans with dense seating:** more people per square foot, more shared surfaces
- **Shared kitchens and snack areas:** heavy, all-day use, often the messiest room in the building
- **Hardware everywhere:** laptops, monitors, docks, dev kits and prototypes on desks
- **Flexible hours:** people arrive late, stay late and work weekends
- **Server rooms, labs and network closets** that should not be entered casually
- **Hybrid schedules:** some days packed, others nearly empty

## Coworking spaces add another layer

Coworking operators host many companies, sometimes dozens, in one space. Members expect hotel-like common areas, and operators need cleaning that:

- Keeps lounges, kitchens and phone booths spotless for members and tours
- Respects private offices rented by member companies
- Handles high restroom and kitchen use
- Works around members using the space at night
- Gives the operator control over who holds keys and codes

## Never touching hardware

Our team does not move, unplug, wipe or otherwise handle laptops, monitors, keyboards, docks, prototypes or any device. Desks are wiped only where clear. Cables on floors are vacuumed around, not moved. That rule protects your equipment and your data.

## Security for equipment and data

Tech companies often have security programs that cover physical access. Frameworks such as NIST's physical and environmental protection controls address who can enter facilities and sensitive areas. Your cleaning vendor sits inside that boundary every night.

- Background-checked, assigned team members
- Credentials logged to individuals, with lost credentials reported immediately
- Server rooms, network closets and labs excluded unless you specify otherwise
- No photography inside client spaces
- Lock-up and alarm checks every visit

See [security and access control](/security-and-access-control).

## Scheduling around late workers

Tech teams often work into the evening. We clean occupied areas last, work quietly around people still at their desks, and can schedule later windows or early-morning service for buildings that rarely empty. For hybrid offices, we concentrate service on the nights after the busiest in-office days. See [recurring janitorial schedules](/services/recurring-janitorial-schedules).

## Kitchens deserve special attention

In a tech office or coworking space, the kitchen is the busiest room. The CDC notes that routine cleaning removes most germs from surfaces, and high-touch kitchen surfaces such as refrigerator handles, coffee machine buttons and faucet handles benefit from frequent attention. Kitchen scopes in these buildings are typically nightly regardless of overall frequency.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will your cleaners touch our computers or devices?

No. Our team never moves, unplugs or wipes hardware. Desks are wiped only where clear.

### Can you clean while people are still working?

Yes. We work quietly around late workers and clean occupied areas last, or schedule later or early-morning visits.

### Do you clean coworking member offices?

Yes, as each member's agreement or the operator specifies, with private offices handled under their own rules.

### Do you enter server rooms?

Not unless you specifically authorize it. Server rooms, network closets and labs are excluded by default.
