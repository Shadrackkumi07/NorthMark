Northmark provides commercial property maintenance for building owners and property managers in Fargo, North Dakota. We perform scheduled condition walkthroughs, handle general upkeep tasks agreed in the scope and deliver written reports with photos. Items that need a licensed trade go to your contractors with clear documentation. Fargo's freeze-thaw winters, spring river season and summer storms shape every maintenance calendar we write.

## Maintenance on Fargo's terms

A maintenance plan written for a mild climate does not fit Fargo. Buildings here go through:

- **Long, deep cold.** Extended subzero stretches stress doors, closers, weatherstripping, sealants and anything with water in it.
- **Freeze-thaw cycles.** Late fall and early spring swings around freezing open cracks in paving, sidewalks, masonry and sealant joints.
- **Spring melt and river season.** The Red River at Fargo is monitored by a USGS gauge, and high-water years put lower levels, sump systems and grading to the test.
- **Summer severe weather.** The National Weather Service office in Grand Forks issues hail, wind and severe thunderstorm warnings for the area most summers.

A Fargo maintenance calendar plans for each season instead of reacting to it.

## A seasonal Fargo maintenance calendar

| Season | Typical walkthrough focus |
|---|---|
| Late fall (Oct–Nov) | Weatherstripping and door sweeps, entry mats ready, roof drains and gutters observed clear, exterior lighting, sidewalk trip hazards before snow hides them |
| Winter (Dec–Mar) | Entry conditions, doors and closers in the cold, signs of ice damming or interior leaks, frozen-pipe risk areas observed, salt damage at entries |
| Spring (Mar–May) | Water intrusion in lower levels, grading and downspout discharge, winter damage to paving and curbs, roof drainage after melt, salt residue cleanup |
| Summer (Jun–Aug) | Post-storm checks, exterior sealants and caulking, landscaping encroachment on buildings, roof documentation before and after hail season |

Each walkthrough produces a report: what was checked, what was found, what we fixed within scope and what needs your trades. See [property condition reports](/services/property-condition-reports).

## What we handle and what goes to your trades

Under a typical Fargo maintenance scope, Northmark handles general upkeep tasks agreed in writing, such as replacing failed lamps in reachable fixtures, adjusting doors and hardware, replacing worn mats, minor touch-ups and similar work. We document and report everything else.

Electrical, plumbing, HVAC, roofing, fire and life safety systems, and structural work go to your licensed contractors. We do not perform those trades or mark them up inside our invoices. Clear handoffs save money: your plumber arrives knowing the location, symptoms and photos instead of spending the first hour investigating.

## Fargo building stock

Fargo's commercial buildings range widely:

- **Downtown masonry buildings** with older roofs, windows and mechanical systems that reward close attention
- **Post-war and 1970s–1990s commercial buildings** in established corridors, often due for envelope and paving work
- **Newer south Fargo and I-29 corridor buildings** where owners want to protect finishes and warranties
- **New construction.** The city issued 61 new commercial building permits valued at about $196 million for January–September 2025, and new buildings benefit from a documented baseline from day one

## Frequently asked questions

### Do you provide property maintenance throughout Fargo?

Yes. We serve commercial properties throughout Fargo, as well as West Fargo and Moorhead.

### Do you do electrical, plumbing or HVAC repairs?

No. We document and report items that require a licensed trade so your contractors can handle them.

### How often should a Fargo commercial property be walked?

Most owners choose monthly or quarterly walkthroughs, with seasonal checks added for late fall, winter and spring thaw.

### Can you prepare our building for winter?

Yes. Our late-fall walkthrough focuses on doors, weatherstripping, entries, drainage and trip hazards before snow arrives.
