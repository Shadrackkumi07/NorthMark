## Flying in Fargo: the airspace question

Fargo is home to Hector International Airport, which handles commercial airline service along with general aviation and military activity. Controlled airspace surrounds it, and that airspace covers a meaningful share of the city's commercial properties, particularly in north Fargo, around the airport and along nearby corridors. Many other parts of the city are close enough that every mission needs an airspace check.

Under Part 107, flying in controlled airspace requires FAA authorization. For most routine commercial missions that authorization is requested through LAANC (Low Altitude Authorization and Notification Capability), which can approve flights up to set altitude limits that vary by location on FAA facility maps. In some spots near the airport, permitted altitudes are low or authorization requires additional steps.

For you, that means:

- We check every Fargo address before quoting or scheduling
- We tell you up front if a site has altitude limits that affect what can be captured
- We obtain authorization before the flight, never after
- We do not fly where it would not be legal or safe

## Fargo weather and when to fly

Fargo's climate shapes the most useful times for aerial documentation:

| Season | Common Fargo missions | Flight considerations |
|---|---|---|
| Spring (after melt) | Roof condition after winter, drainage, paving damage | Wind is often strong; we pick calm windows |
| Summer | Hail and storm documentation, construction progress, facades | Thunderstorms and heat; early-morning flights common |
| Fall | Pre-winter roof and drain check, site surveys before snow | Shorter days; good visibility |
| Winter | Snow load observation, ice dams, emergency documentation | Cold affects batteries and pilots; limited windows |

NOAA's Storm Events Database records hail and severe thunderstorm events across Cass County. Post-storm documentation is one of our most requested Fargo missions. ## What we do and do not provide

We provide dated, organized imagery and a summary of visible observations. We do not diagnose roof systems, estimate repairs, adjust insurance claims or certify condition. Roofers, engineers and adjusters can use our imagery to do those jobs better.

## Planning around downtown Fargo

Downtown Fargo presents its own flight challenges: buildings close together, overhead lines, trees along Broadway and steady street traffic. Before scheduling a downtown mission we review the block, identify launch spots that keep the aircraft away from people and vehicles, and plan the timing, often early morning, to reduce activity on sidewalks and streets. If a particular elevation cannot be captured safely, we tell you before the flight rather than delivering incomplete imagery.
