Commercial cleaning in Fargo–Moorhead is priced mainly by labor: how many hours it takes to clean your building to the agreed scope, multiplied by a rate that covers wages, payroll costs, supplies, supervision, insurance and overhead. The biggest drivers are visit frequency, cleanable square footage, restroom and fixture count, floor types, occupancy and scope detail. Because those vary so much, honest providers price from a walkthrough. This guide explains the variables and walks through an illustrative calculation so you can judge proposals.

## Why phone quotes are unreliable

Two 8,000-square-foot buildings can need very different amounts of work. One is a quiet engineering office with two restrooms and carpet throughout. The other is a busy clinic with six restrooms, hard floors, exam rooms and a waiting room that fills all day. The clinic may need twice the labor. A per-square-foot quote given without seeing either building is a guess, and guesses either overcharge you or set up a crew to cut corners.

## The main cost drivers

| Driver | Why it matters | Effect on cost |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency | Five nights costs more than three | Largest single factor |
| Cleanable square footage | More area takes more time | Roughly proportional, but not linear |
| Restrooms and fixtures | Restrooms are the most labor-intensive areas | High |
| Floor types | Hard floors need mopping; carpet needs vacuuming | Moderate |
| Occupancy and traffic | More people mean more trash, soil and restroom use | Moderate to high |
| Scope detail | Desk wiping, interior glass, kitchens, high dusting | Moderate |
| Access and security requirements | Escorts, restricted rooms, alarm procedures | Low to moderate |
| Building layout | Many small rooms take longer than open plans | Moderate |
| Season | Fargo winters add entry and floor work | Low to moderate |
| Consumables | Whether paper, soap and liners are included | Varies |

## Labor: the core of every price

Labor typically makes up the largest share of a janitorial price. In the Fargo metro, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported a **mean wage of $20.11 an hour** for building and grounds cleaning and maintenance occupations in May 2025, slightly above the national mean of $19.66. On top of wages, an employer pays payroll taxes, workers' compensation, any benefits and paid time off, plus training and turnover costs.

A provider's hourly billing rate must also cover:

- Supervision and QA inspections
- Cleaning chemicals, equipment and equipment replacement
- Liability insurance and bonding
- Vehicles and travel between sites
- Background checks and training
- Administration, scheduling and account management
- Profit

That is why a fully loaded billing rate is always well above the wage a cleaner takes home.

## Production rates: how hours are estimated

Providers estimate hours using production rates, meaning how many square feet a trained cleaner can service per hour for a given space type and task list. Industry rules of thumb for routine office cleaning are often quoted in the low thousands of square feet per hour, with restrooms, clinics and detailed scopes much slower, and open, lightly used spaces faster. Rates vary widely by building, which is another reason walkthroughs matter.

## An illustrative calculation

> The figures below are a hypothetical example for explanation only. They are not a Northmark quote or a market average.

Imagine a 10,000-square-foot Fargo office with 40 employees, four restrooms, mostly carpet, one breakroom, cleaned three nights a week.

1. **Estimate hours per visit.** Suppose the walkthrough finds the routine scope takes about 3 labor hours per visit, including restrooms, breakroom, trash, vacuuming and entries.
2. **Monthly hours.** 3 hours × 3 visits a week × about 4.33 weeks a month ≈ 39 hours a month.
3. **Add periodic tasks.** Weekly and monthly detail work might add 4 hours a month, for a total of about 43 hours.
4. **Apply a loaded rate.** If a provider's loaded rate in this example were $35–$45 an hour, the monthly price would be roughly $1,500–$1,950.

Change one variable and the answer moves. Five nights instead of three raises the hours. Add a clinic's restrooms and hard floors and the per-visit hours climb. Halve the headcount through hybrid work and the trash and restroom load drop. That sensitivity is the whole point: the price follows the work.

## Why the cheapest bid is often the most expensive

When a proposal comes in far below others, the bidder has usually assumed fewer hours than the work needs. The gap shows up as:

- Tasks that quietly stop happening
- High crew turnover, as underpaid staff leave
- Missed nights
- A price increase a few months in
- Your staff time spent managing complaints

Compare proposals by **hours and scope**, not just the monthly total. Ask each bidder how many labor hours per week their price assumes. Our guide to [janitorial pricing per square foot](/resources/janitorial-pricing-per-square-foot) explains why per-square-foot comparisons can mislead.

## Moorhead and Minnesota sales tax

Minnesota taxes certain building cleaning and maintenance services, while North Dakota generally does not tax services the same way. A Moorhead facility's invoice may therefore include sales tax that a comparable Fargo building's would not. Confirm with your tax advisor.

## How to reduce cost without losing quality

- **Use a split schedule:** restrooms, kitchens and trash nightly, full cleaning less often. See [recurring janitorial schedules](/services/recurring-janitorial-schedules).
- **Match frequency to occupancy**, especially with hybrid work.
- **Clarify consumables:** supplying your own paper and soap can simplify billing.
- **Reduce clutter:** clear desks and floors let cleaners work faster.
- **Write a clear scope**, so you pay only for what you need. Use our [scope of work template](/resources/janitorial-scope-of-work-template).

If you are weighing an in-house custodian against a contractor, see [in-house vs outsourced janitorial](/resources/in-house-vs-outsourced-janitorial).

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does office cleaning cost per month in Fargo?

It depends on size, frequency, restrooms and scope. Small offices cleaned once or twice a week cost far less than large or high-traffic buildings cleaned nightly. A walkthrough gives an accurate number.

### What is the average hourly wage for cleaners in Fargo?

BLS reported a mean wage of $20.11 an hour for building and grounds cleaning and maintenance occupations in the Fargo metro in May 2025. Contractor billing rates are higher because they cover payroll costs, supervision, supplies, insurance and overhead.

### Why do commercial cleaning quotes vary so much?

Bidders assume different scopes and labor hours. Compare proposals by weekly labor hours and written scope, not just the monthly price.

### Is commercial cleaning taxed in Moorhead?

Minnesota taxes certain building cleaning and maintenance services. Consult your tax advisor about your situation.
