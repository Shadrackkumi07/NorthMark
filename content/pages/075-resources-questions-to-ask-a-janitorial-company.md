The most useful questions to ask a janitorial company cover seven areas: trust credentials, staffing, scope, quality control, security, pricing and contract terms. Below are 30 specific questions, with notes on what a strong answer sounds like. Use them in interviews, add them to an RFP, or bring them to a walkthrough. A provider that runs a disciplined operation will answer most of them quickly and specifically.

## Trust and credentials

1. **Can you send a current certificate of insurance?** Look for general liability and workers' compensation at minimum. The SBA lists these among common business coverages.
2. **Are you bonded, and what does the bond cover?** A good answer names the bond type and explains it plainly. See [bonded vs insured](/resources/bonded-vs-insured-cleaning-company).
3. **Will you name us as an additional insured if our lease requires it?** The answer should be a simple yes.
4. **Is every person who will enter our building background-checked?** Listen for "every person," not "most." See [janitorial background checks](/resources/janitorial-background-checks).
5. **How long have you served buildings like ours, and can we speak with two of those clients?** References from similar buildings are worth more than general ones.

## Staffing

6. **Will the same people clean our building each visit?** Assigned teams learn the building. Rotating crews do not.
7. **How do you cover absences?** There should be a defined backup plan.
8. **How are new team members trained on our site?** Look for site-specific training on scope, security and safety.
9. **Who supervises the cleaners, and how often do supervisors visit?** A name and a frequency are good signs.
10. **How do you handle staff turnover on an account?** You want advance notice and introductions, not surprises.

## Scope

11. **Will you walk our building before quoting?** A provider that quotes without a walkthrough is guessing.
12. **Can we see a sample written scope?** It should list tasks by area and frequency. See our [scope of work template](/resources/janitorial-scope-of-work-template).
13. **What is not included?** Clear exclusions prevent arguments later.
14. **How are periodic tasks, such as monthly or quarterly work, scheduled and tracked?** "As needed" usually means "rarely."
15. **Who supplies consumables like paper and soap?** Make sure the answer is in writing.

## Quality control

16. **How do you know the work was done each visit?** Look for checklists and visit logs.
17. **How do you inspect quality, and against what standard?** Strong answers describe a rubric and a schedule. See [janitorial QA inspection scoring](/resources/janitorial-qa-inspection-scoring).
18. **Can we see inspection results?** Yes should be the answer.
19. **What happens when we report a problem?** Listen for logging, correction, verification and confirmation.
20. **What do you do when the same problem keeps happening?** Good answers involve finding the cause, not just apologizing.

## Security and access

21. **Who will hold our keys and alarm codes?** Names and roles, not "the crew."
22. **How are keys and codes logged and recovered?** See [vendor key and access control](/resources/vendor-key-and-access-control).
23. **What happens if a key is lost?** You should be told immediately.
24. **How do you verify the building is locked when you leave?** A lock-up checklist is the right answer.
25. **What will your team do if they find signs of a break-in or a leak at night?** They should follow an escalation procedure you approve.

## Safety

26. **How are your staff trained on the chemicals they use?** OSHA's Hazard Communication standard requires training and access to safety data sheets.
27. **Will you follow our site safety rules and PPE requirements?** Essential in warehouses, plants and clinics.

## Pricing and contract

28. **How many labor hours per week does your price assume?** This is the most revealing question on the list. See [janitorial pricing per square foot](/resources/janitorial-pricing-per-square-foot).
29. **How and when can the price change?** Look for a clear process tied to scope changes or a defined review date.
30. **What are the term and cancellation terms?** Reasonable notice periods on both sides are a sign of confidence. See [janitorial contract terms](/resources/janitorial-contract-terms).

## How to score the answers

| Answer quality | What it sounds like | What it suggests |
|---|---|---|
| Strong | Specific, immediate, offers documents | Disciplined operation |
| Adequate | General but willing to follow up in writing | Possibly fine; verify |
| Weak | Vague, defensive or "we've never had a problem" | Process probably does not exist |

A vendor does not need perfect answers to every question, but the pattern matters. Providers who answer security, quality and labor-hour questions specifically tend to deliver consistently.

## Using these questions in an RFP

Copy the questions into your request for proposal and require written answers. Pair them with a written scope and a mandatory walkthrough. For the broader process, see [how to choose a commercial cleaning company](/resources/how-to-choose-a-commercial-cleaning-company).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the most important question to ask a cleaning company?

How many labor hours per week their price assumes. It shows whether the bid can realistically deliver your scope.

### Should I ask for references?

Yes, preferably from clients with buildings similar to yours.

### What documents should I request?

A certificate of insurance, bond details if bonded, a description of background checks, a sample scope and a sample contract.

### Can I use these questions in an RFP?

Yes. Require written answers alongside a written scope and a walkthrough.
