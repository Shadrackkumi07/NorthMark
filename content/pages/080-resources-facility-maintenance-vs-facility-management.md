Facility maintenance is the hands-on work of keeping a building and its systems in working order: inspections, upkeep, repairs and preventive tasks. Facility management is the broader discipline of planning and coordinating everything a building needs to support the organization inside it, including maintenance, cleaning, security, space, vendors, budgets and long-term planning. Maintenance is a function within facility management. Property maintenance is similar to facility maintenance but is usually bought by owners to protect the asset rather than by occupants to support operations.

## The definitions

**Facility management.** The International Facility Management Association describes facility management as a profession that integrates people, place, process and technology to ensure the built environment supports the organization. The Bureau of Labor Statistics groups facilities managers with administrative services managers, who plan, direct and coordinate supportive services, including building maintenance, security and space.

**Facility maintenance.** The BLS describes general maintenance and repair workers as people who fix and maintain machines, mechanical equipment and buildings, including tasks like repairing doors, replacing fixtures and painting. Facility maintenance is the scheduled and reactive work that keeps a building functional.

**Property maintenance.** Practically the same activities as facility maintenance, viewed from the owner's side: protecting the value and condition of a real estate asset, often across several tenants.

## Side-by-side comparison

| | Facility maintenance | Facility management | Property maintenance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core question | Is it working? | Does the building support the organization? | Is the asset in good condition? |
| Typical buyer | Operations or office manager | Executive, facilities director | Owner or property manager |
| Activities | Inspections, upkeep, repairs, preventive tasks | Planning, budgeting, vendor management, space, safety, sustainability, maintenance oversight | Upkeep of common areas, envelope, site; tenant issues |
| Time horizon | Days to months | Months to years | Months to years |
| Output | Working building | Strategy, budgets, coordinated services | Protected asset value |
| Example | Fixing a door closer | Deciding to replace all door hardware next year and who does it | Ensuring common-area doors are maintained for tenants |

## How they fit together

Think of a mid-size Fargo company occupying a 40,000-square-foot building:

- The **facility manager** sets the budget, chooses the cleaning and maintenance vendors, plans a roof replacement in three years and makes sure safety inspections happen.
- The **facility maintenance** provider walks the building monthly, adjusts doors, replaces lamps, reports a slow roof drain and hands electrical issues to the electrician.
- The **janitorial** provider cleans nightly and reports leaks and damage it sees.

If the building is leased, the landlord's **property maintenance** program handles the shared envelope, roof and site.

## Which do you need?

| Situation | What you need |
|---|---|
| Small business in a leased suite | Janitorial service; the landlord handles property maintenance |
| Mid-size business owning its building, no facilities staff | Facility maintenance plus janitorial, with someone coordinating; possibly outsourced facility management support |
| Large organization with many buildings | A facility manager or team, plus maintenance and janitorial vendors |
| Investor owning a few commercial buildings | Property maintenance and condition reporting, often through a property manager |

## Common confusion in proposals

Some vendors call themselves "facility management companies" when they mainly provide cleaning, while others offer true integrated facility management covering many trades. When comparing proposals, ask:

1. Which services do you perform with your own staff?
2. Which do you subcontract?
3. Who coordinates and plans, and who just performs tasks?
4. What reporting will we receive?

## Frequently asked questions

### Is facility maintenance part of facility management?

Yes. Facility management is the broader discipline that plans and coordinates maintenance along with other building services.

### What does a facility maintenance technician do?

Inspections, routine upkeep, preventive tasks and repairs within their skills, such as door hardware, fixtures, lamps and minor repairs, plus reporting items for licensed trades.

### Do small businesses need facility management?

Rarely a full-time facility manager. Most need janitorial service, some maintenance support and someone to coordinate it.

### Is property maintenance the same as facility maintenance?

The activities overlap heavily. Property maintenance is usually bought by owners to protect the asset, while facility maintenance is bought by occupants to support operations.
