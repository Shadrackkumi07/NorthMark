An insured cleaning company carries insurance, mainly general liability and workers' compensation, that pays for accidental property damage or injuries caused by its work and for injuries to its own employees. A bonded cleaning company has a bond, usually a janitorial or fidelity bond, that responds to losses caused by dishonest acts of its employees, such as theft, subject to the bond's terms. Insurance covers accidents; a bond addresses dishonesty. Commercial buyers should require insurance from every vendor and value bonding as an added protection.

## What "insured" usually means

The SBA describes several common business insurance types. For a cleaning company the most relevant are:

| Coverage | Protects against | Example |
|---|---|---|
| General liability | Third-party bodily injury and property damage caused by the company's operations | A cleaner knocks over and breaks a monitor, or a visitor slips on a freshly mopped floor |
| Workers' compensation | Injuries to the company's own employees on the job | A cleaner strains a back lifting a trash bin at your site |
| Commercial auto | Accidents involving business vehicles | A company van damages your gate |
| Umbrella or excess | Claims above primary policy limits | A large injury claim |

Workers' compensation matters more than buyers often realize. If an uninsured contractor's worker is injured on your premises, questions about who pays can come back to you.

## What "bonded" usually means

A bond is a three-party arrangement among the cleaning company, a surety and the protected party. The SBA's surety bond resources explain the general structure. In cleaning, the common types are:

- **Janitorial service bond:** responds if an employee of the cleaning company steals from a client, typically after the claim is proven, often by conviction or other evidence the bond requires
- **Fidelity or employee dishonesty coverage:** similar protection, sometimes structured as insurance

Bonds have conditions. Coverage limits can be modest, and proof requirements can be strict. Ask what the bond covers, its limit and what a claim requires.

## Bonded vs insured at a glance

| | Insured | Bonded |
|---|---|---|
| Main purpose | Accidents and injuries | Dishonest acts by employees |
| Who it mainly protects | Third parties, clients, the company's workers | Clients |
| Typical requirement | Almost always required by commercial clients | Valued, sometimes required |
| Proof for claim | Accident and damages | Often proof of dishonest act |

## Neither replaces good practices

Insurance and bonds pay after something goes wrong. Prevention matters more:

- **Background checks** for everyone with access. See [janitorial background checks](/resources/janitorial-background-checks)
- **Assigned staff** rather than rotating crews
- **Logged keys and codes** with lock-up verification. See [vendor key and access control](/resources/vendor-key-and-access-control)
- **Supervision** that checks behavior as well as cleaning

## How to verify

1. **Request a certificate of insurance (COI)** directly from the vendor or its agent. Check the named insured, policy dates, coverage types and limits.
2. **Ask to be named as additional insured** if your lease or policy requires it.
3. **Request bond details** in writing: type, surety, limit and claim conditions.
4. **Recheck at renewal.** Policies lapse.
5. **Call the agent** listed on the certificate if anything looks wrong.

The NAIC's consumer resources explain insurance basics if you want more background.

## What to put in your contract

- Required coverage types and minimum limits
- Additional insured wording
- Certificates at signing and each renewal
- Notice if coverage is cancelled
- Bond details if the vendor represents itself as bonded

See [janitorial contract terms](/resources/janitorial-contract-terms).

## Red flags

- "We're covered" with no certificate
- A certificate that names a different company
- Expired policy dates
- No workers' compensation
- "Bonded" claimed with no bond details

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it better to hire a bonded or an insured cleaning company?

Ideally both. Insurance is essential for any commercial vendor. Bonding adds protection against employee dishonesty.

### Does a bond cover accidental damage?

Generally no. Accidental damage is a general liability insurance matter. Bonds focus on dishonest acts.

### How do I check if a cleaning company is insured?

Request a certificate of insurance and confirm the named insured, dates, coverage types and limits. Call the agent if unsure.

### Does workers' compensation protect my business?

It covers the vendor's injured employees, which helps keep those claims from falling on you or your insurance.
