Most offices should be professionally cleaned between two and five times a week. Offices with fewer than about 15 people and few visitors can often manage with one or two visits, offices of 15 to 50 people usually need three to five, and busy or customer-facing offices need five. The better answer, though, is to set frequency by area: restrooms, kitchens, trash and entries need attention far more often than desk dusting or detail vacuuming. A split schedule built that way usually gives the cleanest result for the money.

## Why one number does not fit every office

"How often" depends on how fast each area gets dirty and how much it matters when it does. A 40-person office's restroom may need daily attention, while its conference room barely changes between Tuesday and Thursday. Treating the whole office on one frequency either overpays for low-impact areas or underserves high-impact ones.

The CDC's facility cleaning guidance makes a related point about germs: routine cleaning of frequently touched surfaces matters most, with frequency increased where more people share a space. OSHA's sanitation standard also requires that workplace toilet facilities be kept clean and sanitary, which is a practical argument for frequent restroom service in any occupied office.

## Frequency by area

| Area | Low traffic (under 15 people) | Moderate (15–50 people) | High traffic or customer-facing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Restrooms | 2 times a week | 3–5 times a week | Daily |
| Kitchen or breakroom | 2 times a week | 3–5 times a week | Daily |
| Trash and recycling | 2 times a week | 3–5 times a week | Daily |
| Entry, lobby and reception | 1–2 times a week | 3–5 times a week | Daily |
| Vacuuming traffic areas | 1–2 times a week | 3 times a week | Daily |
| Dusting cleared surfaces | Weekly | Weekly | Weekly or twice weekly |
| Detail vacuuming and edges | Every two weeks | Weekly | Weekly |
| High dusting, vents, baseboards | Monthly | Monthly | Monthly |

These are starting points. A walkthrough refines them for your building.

## Factors that push frequency up

- **Headcount density.** More people per restroom and per square foot means faster soiling.
- **Visitors.** Clients, patients or the public raise the standard and the traffic.
- **Food.** Catered meetings, shared kitchens and eating at desks add trash and spills.
- **Hard floors in high-visibility areas.** They show soil quickly.
- **Health sensitivity.** Clinics, childcare and senior-serving spaces need more frequent attention to touchpoints and restrooms.
- **Winter.** Fargo–Moorhead entries take snow, slush and salt for months. See [winter facility care in Fargo](/resources/winter-facility-care-fargo).

## Factors that let frequency come down

- **Hybrid schedules.** If the office is busy Tuesday through Thursday, concentrate service on those nights and the morning after.
- **Few restrooms but low use.** A small office with one restroom and eight people does not need nightly service.
- **Clean-desk habits.** Clear desks and floors make each visit faster and more effective.
- **Good matting.** Proper walk-off mats at entries reduce soil spread.

## Hybrid offices: a practical approach

Many Fargo–Moorhead offices now have two or three heavy days a week. A sensible plan:

1. Full cleaning on the evening of the last busy day of the week.
2. Restrooms, kitchen and trash after each busy day.
3. Light or no visits on near-empty days.

That often saves money compared with a flat five-night plan without any loss of perceived cleanliness.

## Signs your frequency is too low

- Trash smells or overflows before the next visit
- Restroom complaints, especially late in the day before cleaning night
- Visible dust on surfaces visitors see
- Gritty entry floors and stained carpet near doors
- Staff cleaning the kitchen themselves

## Signs it may be too high

- Cleaners report little to do in some areas
- Several areas are rarely used
- Occupancy has dropped since the schedule was set

## Cleaning and indoor air

Dust and debris affect how an office feels. The EPA's guide for office building occupants lists housekeeping among the factors that influence indoor air quality. Regular vacuuming with well-maintained equipment and prompt trash removal help, especially in offices with carpet and high occupancy.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should a small office be cleaned?

Most offices under about 15 people do well with one or two professional visits a week, with restrooms and kitchen attended each visit.

### Should restrooms be cleaned daily?

In offices with moderate or heavy use, yes. In small, low-use offices, two or three times a week is usually enough.

### Is weekly office cleaning enough?

For very small or lightly used offices, often yes. Most offices with regular daily occupancy need more.

### How does hybrid work change cleaning frequency?

Concentrate cleaning on the evenings after your busiest in-office days and reduce visits on near-empty days.
