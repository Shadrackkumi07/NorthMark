This office cleaning checklist lists the tasks most offices need, grouped by area and frequency: every visit, weekly, monthly and seasonal. Use it to write a janitorial scope, to brief an in-house cleaner, or to check whether your current vendor is doing what you pay for. Adjust frequencies to your headcount, visitors and layout. The every-visit list assumes service several times a week.

## How to use this checklist

1. **Mark which areas exist** in your office and delete the rest.
2. **Set frequencies** using our guide to [how often an office should be cleaned](/resources/how-often-should-an-office-be-cleaned).
3. **Add site specifics**: restricted rooms, product preferences, recycling rules, alarm procedures.
4. **Write it into a scope** so it becomes the standard your vendor is inspected against. Our [scope of work template](/resources/janitorial-scope-of-work-template) shows the format.

## Entry, reception and lobby

**Every visit**

- Clean interior and exterior entry door glass within reach
- Wipe door handles and push plates
- Vacuum or mop entry floor; shake out or vacuum mats
- Wipe reception counter and visitor seating arms
- Empty trash

**Weekly**

- Dust reception furniture, frames and decor within reach
- Detail mats and entry corners

**Monthly**

- Baseboards, door frames and kick plates
- High dusting of ledges and vents within safe reach

## Workstations and private offices

**Every visit**

- Empty trash and recycling; replace liners as needed
- Vacuum traffic lanes
- Spot-clean visible spills on hard floors

**Weekly**

- Dust cleared desk surfaces, shelves and filing cabinet tops (papers stay untouched)
- Detail vacuum under desks and along edges
- Wipe chair arms and phone exteriors where cleared and approved

**Monthly**

- Dust blinds and window sills
- High dusting of vents and light fixtures within reach
- Baseboards

## Conference rooms

**Every visit**

- Wipe tables
- Reset chairs
- Empty trash
- Clean whiteboard trays
- Vacuum or mop

**Weekly**

- Dust credenzas and AV furniture exteriors
- Spot-clean glass walls and doors

## Restrooms

**Every visit**

- Clean and disinfect toilets, urinals, sinks and counters
- Clean mirrors
- Wipe dispensers, door handles, flush handles and faucet handles
- Empty waste and sanitary receptacles
- Mop floor with appropriate product
- Report or refill supplies according to the contract
- Report leaks, running fixtures or broken dispensers

**Weekly**

- Wipe partitions and doors
- Detail walls around fixtures
- Scrub floor corners and edges

**Monthly**

- Clean vents and light lenses within reach
- Detail baseboards

## Kitchen and breakroom

**Every visit**

- Clean counters, sink and faucet
- Wipe tables and chair seats
- Wipe exteriors of refrigerator, microwave, coffee maker and dishwasher
- Empty trash, recycling and compost
- Mop floor

**Weekly**

- Clean microwave interior
- Wipe cabinet fronts and handles

**Monthly**

- Wipe the top of the refrigerator and other appliance tops within reach
- Detail baseboards

## Corridors, stairs and elevators

**Every visit**

- Vacuum or dust-mop corridors
- Spot-clean walls and doors at hand height
- Wipe elevator buttons and handrails

**Weekly**

- Sweep or vacuum stairs; wipe handrails
- Clean elevator walls and door tracks

## Touchpoints (every visit)

- Door handles and push plates
- Light switches
- Elevator buttons
- Shared printer and copier exteriors (never interior or controls unless approved)
- Handrails

The CDC notes that routine cleaning removes most germs from surfaces, so frequently touched surfaces deserve regular attention.

## Seasonal tasks for Fargo–Moorhead offices

**Late fall to spring (roughly November to April)**

- Extra entry floor cleaning to remove salt residue and grit
- More frequent mat changes or vacuuming
- Check vestibules and the first 20 feet inside each entrance every visit

**Spring**

- Detail entries and walk-off areas after winter
- Wash interior entry glass edges and frames

See [winter facility care in Fargo](/resources/winter-facility-care-fargo).

## Safety and products

- Keep safety data sheets for every product on site. OSHA's Hazard Communication standard requires access and training.
- Use wet floor signs during and after mopping.
- Match products to surfaces and to any preferences, such as fragrance-free products.
- Consider products carrying the EPA Safer Choice label where they meet your needs.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should be on a daily office cleaning checklist?

Trash and recycling, restroom cleaning, kitchen counters and sink, entry glass and floors, touchpoints and vacuuming traffic areas.

### How often should office desks be cleaned?

Cleared desk surfaces are usually dusted weekly. Papers and personal items are left untouched.

### Should office cleaning include disinfecting?

Restroom fixtures and frequently touched surfaces are commonly disinfected. Other surfaces usually only need routine cleaning.

### Can I use this checklist for my cleaning contract?

Yes. Adapt it to your office and write it into your scope of work.
