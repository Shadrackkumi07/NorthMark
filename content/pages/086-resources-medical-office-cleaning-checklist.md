This medical office cleaning checklist covers the janitorial side of a clinic: waiting rooms, reception, restrooms, corridors, offices, staff rooms and the exam room surfaces a practice designates, with frequencies for each. It also lists what belongs to clinical staff rather than the janitorial vendor, such as exam tables, equipment, sharps and regulated waste, plus product and privacy rules. Adapt it to your practice and attach it to your janitorial scope.

## Before you start: agree the boundaries

The CDC's environmental infection control guidelines describe how cleaning of environmental surfaces in healthcare settings should be matched to risk, with frequently touched surfaces in patient areas getting particular attention. OSHA's bloodborne pathogens standard requires employers to protect workers from exposure. Both point to the same practical step: write down who cleans what.

**Clinical staff typically handle:**

- Exam tables, chairs and treatment surfaces between patients
- Medical equipment, devices and instruments
- Sharps containers and regulated medical waste
- Blood and body fluid spills under the practice's exposure control plan
- Medication areas and refrigerators

**The janitorial vendor typically handles:** everything on the checklist below.

## Entry, waiting room and reception

**Every visit**

- Clean entry glass and door hardware
- Vacuum or mop entry and waiting room floors; manage mats
- Wipe seating arms, side tables and magazine racks
- Clean reception counter exterior and patient-side surfaces (not screens, paperwork or devices)
- Wipe touchpoints: handles, switches, push plates, pens holders if approved
- Empty non-regulated trash

**Weekly**

- Detail seating, including under cushions where accessible
- Dust frames, ledges and decor within reach
- Spot-clean walls at hand height

**Monthly**

- Baseboards, vents and corners

## Patient restrooms

**Every visit**

- Clean and disinfect toilets, sinks, grab bars, counters, handles and dispensers
- Clean mirrors
- Empty waste and sanitary receptacles
- Mop floors with agreed product
- Report or restock supplies per contract
- Report leaks, running fixtures and broken dispensers

**Weekly**

- Partitions, doors and walls around fixtures

## Exam rooms (practice-designated surfaces only)

**Every visit**

- Mop or vacuum floors
- Empty non-regulated trash
- Wipe door handles, light switches and cabinet handles
- Clean sinks and faucets
- Wipe counters only where cleared by clinical staff

**Weekly**

- Spot-clean walls and doors
- Dust ledges and frames within reach

**Never by janitorial staff unless specifically scoped:** exam tables, equipment, sharps, red-bag waste, medication storage.

## Corridors and nurse stations

**Every visit:** floors, handrails, touchpoints, non-clinical counters cleared by staff, trash.
**Weekly:** wall spot-cleaning, door detail.

## Offices, billing and records areas

**Every visit:** trash, floors.
**Weekly:** dust cleared surfaces.
**Always:** do not read, move or photograph documents or screens. Records rooms excluded unless the practice sets conditions.

## Staff breakroom

**Every visit:** counters, sink, tables, appliance exteriors, floor, trash.
**Weekly:** microwave interior, cabinet fronts.

## Products

- Disinfectants used in patient areas are agreed with the practice and EPA-registered
- Follow label contact times; see [cleaning vs sanitizing vs disinfecting](/resources/cleaning-vs-sanitizing-vs-disinfecting)
- Keep vendor products separate from clinical supplies
- Safety data sheets on site for every vendor product
- Fragrance-free products where patients or staff are sensitive

## Privacy

HHS guidance on HIPAA acknowledges that some incidental exposure to health information can occur in normal operations if reasonable safeguards are in place. For cleaning vendors, reasonable safeguards mean screened staff, clear rules against handling records or screens, restricted rooms and confidentiality commitments in the contract.

## Safety

- Team members trained on the practice's procedure if they encounter blood, sharps or potentially infectious material: stop, do not touch, report
- Gloves and appropriate PPE for restroom and waste tasks
- Wet floor signage in patient areas, where many visitors are elderly or mobility-limited

See [OSHA bloodborne pathogens and medical office cleaning](/resources/osha-bloodborne-pathogens-medical-office-cleaning).

## Frequently asked questions

### Who cleans exam tables in a medical office?

Clinical staff, between patients, using the practice's approved products. Janitorial staff clean floors and designated room surfaces.

### Should janitorial staff empty sharps containers?

No. Sharps and regulated medical waste are handled by the practice and its licensed waste hauler.

### How often should a medical office be cleaned?

Most medical offices need janitorial service every day they are open, typically after the last patient.

### What products should be used in patient areas?

EPA-registered products agreed with the practice and used according to label directions, including contact time.
