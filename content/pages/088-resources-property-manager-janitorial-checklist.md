A property manager's janitorial checklist has four parts: the common-area cleaning tasks and their frequencies, the oversight routine that confirms the vendor is doing them, the compliance documents every vendor must provide, and the reporting owners expect. Use the lists below to set up a new building, audit a current vendor, or standardize several properties. They apply to multi-tenant office, retail, medical office and multifamily common areas.

## Part 1: Common-area tasks

### Lobbies and entries

| Task | Frequency |
|---|---|
| Entry glass and door hardware | Daily |
| Entry floors and mats | Daily; more often in winter |
| Directory, reception and mailbox exteriors | Daily |
| Seating and decor dusting | Weekly |
| Baseboards and corners | Monthly |

### Elevators

| Task | Frequency |
|---|---|
| Floors | Daily |
| Panels, buttons and handrails | Daily |
| Walls and doors | Several times a week |
| Door tracks | Weekly |

### Corridors and stairwells

| Task | Frequency |
|---|---|
| Vacuum or mop corridors | Daily to several times a week |
| Touchpoints and light switches | Daily |
| Stairwells swept, handrails wiped | Weekly |
| Exit routes checked for obstructions | Each visit, reported |

### Common restrooms

| Task | Frequency |
|---|---|
| Fixtures, mirrors, counters, floors, waste | Daily |
| Supply restocking or reporting | Daily |
| Partitions and walls | Weekly |
| Vents and light lenses | Monthly |

### Shared amenities

Fitness rooms, conference centers, shared kitchens, mailrooms, laundry rooms and trash rooms each need their own task list. See [multifamily common areas](/industries/multifamily-common-areas) for residential amenities and [multi-tenant office buildings](/industries/multi-tenant-office-buildings) for office amenities.

## Part 2: Vendor oversight routine

- **Monthly walkthrough** of each building with the checklist, scoring each area. See [janitorial QA inspection scoring](/resources/janitorial-qa-inspection-scoring)
- **Review vendor inspection reports** if provided
- **Track tenant complaints** by building and area
- **Quarterly review meeting** with the vendor covering trends and scope changes
- **Spot checks** at different times, including early mornings
- **Exit route check** during each walkthrough. OSHA requires exit routes to stay unobstructed

Industry organizations such as BOMA International and IREM publish resources on building operations that many managers use to benchmark standards.

## Part 3: Compliance documents

Keep a file for each vendor, ideally per property:

- [ ] Signed contract with scope attached
- [ ] Certificate of insurance, current, with correct certificate holder and additional insureds per owner entity
- [ ] Bond details if the vendor claims to be bonded
- [ ] Description of background check process
- [ ] List of personnel with access, and credential log
- [ ] Safety data sheets for products used on site
- [ ] Escalation and emergency contacts
- [ ] Renewal dates for insurance and contract

See [insured and bonded](/insured-and-bonded) and [bonded vs insured](/resources/bonded-vs-insured-cleaning-company).

## Part 4: Tenant issue handling

1. Tenant reports issue to management
2. Management logs it and sends to vendor's account contact
3. Vendor corrects and confirms
4. Management confirms with tenant
5. Repeat issues reviewed monthly

A vendor with a single accountable contact makes this far easier. See [janitorial account management](/services/janitorial-account-management).

## Part 5: Owner reporting

Owners increasingly want evidence. A simple monthly or quarterly owner report can include:

- Inspection scores by building and trend
- Tenant cleaning complaints and resolution times
- Maintenance issues reported by the cleaning vendor
- Upcoming periodic work
- Condition photos. See [property condition reports](/services/property-condition-reports)

## Part 6: Seasonal items for Fargo–Moorhead

- **November to April:** winter entry program, extra mats, salt residue removal, elevator floor attention
- **Spring:** entry detail after winter, lower-level water checks
- **Late summer and fall:** move-in season traffic near colleges, pre-winter walkthrough

## Standardizing across properties

Use one checklist format and one scoring rubric for every building, even if tasks and frequencies differ. That lets you compare properties and spot outliers. See [multi-site janitorial](/services/multi-site-janitorial) and [property management](/industries/property-management). To build the scope itself, use our [scope of work template](/resources/janitorial-scope-of-work-template).

## Setting up a new building

When a new property enters your portfolio, start with a walkthrough using this checklist, mark which areas exist, set frequencies, and request the compliance documents before the vendor's first visit. Run a calibration inspection with the vendor in the first two weeks so both sides agree on what "meets scope" looks like, then move to the regular monthly routine. Buildings set up this way rarely produce the early tenant complaints that otherwise follow a new acquisition.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should common areas be cleaned?

High-traffic lobbies, restrooms and elevators usually need daily service. Corridors and stairwells can often be cleaned several times a week to weekly.

### What documents should property managers collect from cleaning vendors?

A signed contract with scope, current certificates of insurance per property, bond details if applicable, background check information, a personnel list and safety data sheets.

### How should property managers check cleaning quality?

Monthly scored walkthroughs, vendor inspection reports, complaint tracking and quarterly review meetings.

### Should every building use the same checklist?

Use the same format and scoring rubric, but adjust tasks and frequencies to each building.
