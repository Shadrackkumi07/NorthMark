Spring flood season in Fargo–Moorhead runs from snowmelt in March and April through the Red River's crest, and in high-water years it can threaten lower levels, utilities and site drainage of commercial buildings well beyond the river's edge. Preparation means watching the National Weather Service spring flood outlooks, checking lower levels, sump systems, backflow protection, downspouts and grading before melt, documenting condition in advance, moving vulnerable contents up, and having contacts and plans ready. This guide is general preparation advice, not engineering or emergency management guidance.

## Why the Red River floods

The Red River of the North flows north toward Canada through an exceptionally flat valley. Spring melt often begins in the south while the river further north is still frozen, and the flat terrain spreads water widely. The USGS gauge at Fargo records river levels, and Fargo's record crest of about 40.8 feet came in March 2009, part of a history of major floods that have shaped how the region plans. Large flood protection projects, including the Fargo-Moorhead Area Diversion, have been built in response.

Even in years when the river stays within its banks, rapid snowmelt overwhelms storm drains and pushes water toward foundations and lower levels.

## Watch the outlooks

The National Weather Service office in Grand Forks issues spring flood outlooks for the Red River basin, typically starting in late winter, based on snowpack, soil moisture and frost depth. Watching them gives building owners weeks of notice in high-risk years. Know whether your property is in or near a mapped flood hazard area; FEMA's flood maps are the starting point.

## Pre-melt checklist (late winter)

**Lower levels and utilities**

- Inspect basements and lower levels for cracks, prior water marks and efflorescence
- Test sump pumps; confirm discharge lines are clear and directed away from the building
- Confirm backup power or battery backup for sump pumps where installed
- Confirm backflow prevention on sewer connections is in place and functional (a licensed plumber should service it)
- Note the location of utility shutoffs

**Site and drainage**

- Clear snow from around downspout outlets and drains where practical
- Extend downspouts away from foundations
- Note low spots where meltwater collects
- Clear storm drain inlets on the property of ice and debris where safe

**Roof**

- Confirm roof drains and scuppers are not blocked by ice or debris
- Consider a [drone roof inspection](/services/drone-roof-inspection) after the worst of winter

**Contents and records**

- Move valuable contents, records and equipment off lower-level floors
- Back up and relocate critical records stored below grade
- Review insurance; standard commercial property policies often exclude flood, and flood coverage usually has a waiting period

## Document before the water

Dated condition records made before flood season help with insurance claims and contractor assessments afterward. A [property condition report](/services/property-condition-reports) of lower levels, plus aerial imagery of the site, gives a clear baseline.

## During melt and high water

- Check lower levels and sump operation frequently
- Watch for water at foundations and entries
- Follow city and county instructions; Fargo, Moorhead and Cass and Clay counties communicate flood information during high-water events
- Ready.gov's flood guidance covers safety basics, including never entering flooded areas with electrical hazards

## After the water

- Document conditions with dated photos before cleanup
- Contact your insurer promptly
- Use qualified water damage restoration contractors for wet building materials. Northmark does not provide water damage restoration
- Have electrical and mechanical systems checked by licensed trades before re-energizing flooded equipment
- Clean and inspect sump and drainage systems

## A spring walkthrough

Even in dry years, a spring walkthrough is worthwhile: lower-level moisture, foundation cracks, grading changes, winter paving damage and roof drainage. Northmark includes spring checks in [preventive maintenance walkthroughs](/services/preventive-maintenance-walkthroughs) and [commercial property maintenance](/services/commercial-property-maintenance) programs in [Fargo](/locations/fargo-nd/commercial-property-maintenance) and [Moorhead](/locations/moorhead-mn/commercial-property-maintenance).

## Frequently asked questions

### When is flood season in Fargo–Moorhead?

Typically from snowmelt in March and April through the Red River crest, which varies from year to year.

### Does commercial property insurance cover flooding?

Often not. Many commercial property policies exclude flood. Review your coverage with your agent well before spring.

### What should be checked before spring melt?

Lower levels, sump pumps and discharge, backflow protection, downspouts, grading, roof drains and contents stored below grade.

### Does Northmark provide flood cleanup?

No. We provide pre-season walkthroughs and documentation. Water damage restoration should be handled by qualified restoration contractors.
